6. Online (Tele) Consultation and the AI Photo Tool

An online consultation with our doctors is governed by the Telemedicine Practice Guidelines 2020, which have statutory force as Appendix 5 to the Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002. What that means for you:

  • The fee is the same as in person — ₹200 (VIP ₹500) — and a receipt is issued for it. We do not offer a free or discounted first online consultation; soliciting patients for telemedicine by advertisement or inducement is not permitted (clause 3.7.1.4).
  • It is not anonymous. We need to know who you are, and you are entitled to know who the doctor is. The doctor will begin by giving you their name and qualifications (clause 3.2.4). Each branch listing on this website names the doctor or doctors at that branch with their qualification, and gives the medical-council registration number we hold for that branch, so that you can check it on the register (clause 3.2.5). Where a listing carries no number for a doctor — or where the doctor who sees you is not one of those listed — ask for their registration number; you are entitled to it.
  • Your first consultation should be a video call. A doctor can prescribe the restricted lists of medicines on a first consultation only where it is conducted by video (clause 3.7.4). On a chat or audio call the doctor can give advice and over-the-counter medicines only. No prescription is issued without an appropriate diagnosis — that would be professional misconduct.
  • You may be asked to come in instead. Where physical examination is critical, the doctor must not proceed online (clause 3.5.1). Skin, hair and nail problems often need dermoscopy, trichoscopy, scraping or a biopsy. Being asked to visit a branch is the doctor doing their job properly, not a sales tactic.
  • We will never push you online if you want to come in. Insisting on telemedicine when a patient is willing to attend in person is listed as misconduct (clause 3.7.1.4). Ask for an in-person appointment at any of our branches and you will get one.
  • If the patient is under 18, the consultation takes place only with an identified adult present (clause 3.2.3).
  • Procedures are done in person. Hair transplant, laser, botox and similar procedures can be discussed online but are carried out at a branch. Telemedicine does not cover remote procedures.
  • This service is for patients in India. The Guidelines do not provide for consultations outside Indian jurisdiction (clause 1.2).
  • Certain medicines can never be prescribed online — anything in Schedule X of the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, and any narcotic or psychotropic substance under the NDPS Act, 1985. Please do not ask; the answer will be no.
  • The AI photo tool is not a consultation and not a diagnosis. An artificial-intelligence platform is not permitted to counsel a patient or prescribe (clause 5.4), and only a registered medical practitioner may form a clinical opinion (section 34, National Medical Commission Act, 2019). The tool describes what is visible in a photograph; the output is marked as AI-generated; a doctor takes it from there. How your photograph is handled is set out in section 4 of our Privacy Policy.
